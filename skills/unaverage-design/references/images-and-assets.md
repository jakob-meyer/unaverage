# Images and assets

Read in stage 0 (material check) and stage 4, before an image is searched for, built or generated: images are planned before the build. Law: [compliance.md](compliance.md); EU and DACH: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md). Evidence strength: high, medium, H (derivation). As of 2026-10-10.

## 1 Asset plan

| Surface | Ideal motif | Source | Status | Image brief | Credits ID |

| Hero | product as a cutout, 45 degrees, with scale | user (MA) | requested | P1 | photo-own-01 |

- **Status:** available, built, archive, library, requested, approval needed, stand-in, final-form slot.
- **Ideal motif first,** derived from the claim of the surface (the problem, the use, the result), then the source; otherwise it becomes a stock cliché.
- **Origin** from product, name, customer, place, data, material or archive (ground rule G2). Review question: Is this image possible only here? (tell V8)
- **One strong image instead of three middling ones.** The anchor is an image, product, data form or typography, never a row of icons (tells D7, C10).
- **Material gate:** [directions/README.md](directions/README.md) 9. If required material is missing, downgrade the direction; never invent material.
- **Stand-in instead of placeholder:** if material is missing, a best-form stand-in (5) that shows the claim carries the surface until delivery, in final form and final size; if none shows the claim, a final-form slot (5). Label and wanted material go into the review view and the wrap-up ("stand-in: free photo of a building shell with a marked defect; wanted: photo of your own construction site, 1600 x 900"); never ship it unlabeled as finished (ground rule G7).

## 2 Sources in order

**The image source is the user's choice** (Q5, [dialogue.md](dialogue.md) 3.3), asked when a surface plans a photo or a direction in the top 3 needs one and the prompt does not settle it (in M1c or M1a, where the set is not known yet, as a correctable assumption, from category and material). Three sources: no photos (built visuals from the idea, 3.7, 3.8), free photos the skill finds (license per 3.6 and 6, needs web access), or the user's own images, sent or from their website. Images from the website count as the user's material only after the user confirms rights and consent for this use (EU and DACH: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 9, 12); until then the skill lists their addresses in the contract (field 7, the kickoff names only how many) and downloads nothing, stock found there stays stock (5), and a file below the pixel minimum (3.3) is used only at the size it carries. The skill recommends one source first, with its reason, from category, material and the recommended direction ([directions/README.md](directions/README.md) 9), and says in one line what the others lose. By category: a physical product with real product photos (for a coffee roastery the bags and the roast), a service sold through people with real people and their consent, software with a real capture (3.5), and with one every built view follows its look; without one, the mockup from the brief (5). Free photos keep photo directions downgraded; a public-domain archive image whose subject is the subject of the brief (3.6) is material, not stock. On websites with free photos only, the hero and the stage surfaces ([craft.md](craft.md) 5.5) stay with the built idea, and the kickoff says so; an explicit wish for a photo there applies with its loss named, and so does a photo when the built visual would miss illustration quality (5, point 3). On silence and in the modes Autonomous and No user available: the recommendation among built visuals and free photos; own images become an asset request. The choice opens or closes sources; among the open ones the order below applies. Reason: in the skill's own test, free stock photos as the main image pulled pages toward a photo hero with standard sections, while archive plates tied to the subject carried identity; a silent choice can miss what the user has in mind.

1. **The user's material:** project folder, CI, photos, screenshots, data, earlier visuals.
2. **Self-built:** product UI in HTML, data form, SVG technique, type as image, cutouts from own material.
3. **Public-domain archives (CC0),** when the subject carries them: culture, food, science, origin.
4. **Free stock libraries** for context without a claim to authenticity, following an image brief, also as a stand-in (5).
5. **Image generation** only after approval (8).

Photos of real people and products get looked at; stock images get skipped (medium [1]). **Only the user can supply:** real faces, product, data, logo, customer logos, quotes; request them (4), never invent them; until then a stand-in (5) that fakes none of these.

## 3 Getting material cheaply

### 3.1 Cheapest real route per direction

ORD, RUL, MAX and RAW need no outside material; photos improve them.

### 3.2 Phone photo guide

All motifs (medium [2], unless noted otherwise):

1. **Light:** window, motif at 90 degrees to the side, no direct sun (baking paper in front), ceiling light off, never flash; white cardboard opposite as a reflector.
2. **Lock exposure and white balance** for the series; no digital zoom, no front camera.
3. **Distance instead of wide angle** (high [3]): people with the 2x or 3x lens from 1.5 to 2.5 m. Wide angle only for rooms.
4. **The motif's eye level** or a 90-degree top view, camera level indicator on; tripod, same position per series, turn the product instead of the camera.
5. **Full resolution, 4:3,** portrait mode off; crop only in the layout.

**Product:** front, 45 degrees, detail, scale in the hand, in use (setup 3.4). **Team:** one at a time at the same window, same crop, calm background; per person one image looking into the camera, one at work, hands as a reserve; written consent, and how long it holds after someone leaves depends on local law (EU and DACH: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 9). **Place:** an overview plus three to five details; third-party logos, customer data, license plates and bystanders out of the frame. A real phone photo is not a stopgap; reportage instead of staged office photos.

### 3.3 Minimum resolution

Screen: twice the display size (H): a web hero at 1440 px needs 2880 px, a 12 MP photo tolerates only about a 1.4x crop; social 1080 x 1350 from 2160 x 2700. Marketplace (case B5): 2000 px on the long side (medium, check the platform help). Print: 300 dpi at final size (A4 2480 x 3508, US Letter 2550 x 3300). Press kit (case J3): full resolution, portrait and landscape, without text.

### 3.4 Kitchen-table packshot

**Packshot spec** before the photo (STI, ONE, case B5, case I4): view, background (hex), light, shadow (contact, floor, none), final size and fill ratio, visible feature. Cardboard as a sweep; a light product on a mid-gray background, a dark one on white, a glossy one with a white card as a reflection; one shot per view plus one of the empty background. Then locally (tested 2026-10-08; `$S` following [scripts/README.md](../scripts/README.md)):

```bash
mkdir -p ~/.unaverage && swiftc -O $S/cutout.swift -o ~/.unaverage/cutout  # once, macOS 14+
~/.unaverage/cutout photo.jpg subject.png
$PY $S/shadow.py subject.png packshot.jpg --size 1600x1600 --background "#f2f2f0" --light left
$PY $S/shadow.py subject.png dark.jpg --size 1080x1350 --background "#1d1f22" --light right --cast 0.6 --choke 2
$PY $S/shadow.py subject.png market.jpg --size 2000x2000 --background "#ffffff" --fill 0.86 --cast 0 --contact 0
```

- **One light per surface,** all shadows away from the bright side. `shadow.py` builds only contact and floor shadows; a shape-accurate cast shadow comes only from a photo on the final background (medium, the skill's own test).
- **Check edges at 200%:** thin parts, low-contrast edges, a light fringe on a dark background (`--choke 2`), hair, glass.
- **Marketplace:** background 255, no text, only parts that come with the product; contact shadow only when the platform allows it.
- **Truth:** color, form and surface as in reality (no misleading product images; EU and DACH: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 13). A sticker border (white, 6 to 12 px) only when the tone carries it, else tell V20.

### 3.5 Product screenshots

The best PRO material (reference: Linear).

- **Data:** a demo account with one continuous case story (one company, one week); names from the target market, odd values with units. Never customer data, Acme, Lorem or round fantasy numbers (tell E5); user data only after approval, personal data blacked out.
- **State after the action,** benefit visible, one action per image, banners gone. **A crop instead of the whole screen.**
- **Double resolution:** `node $S/render.mjs <url|file> <folder> --viewports 1440x900 --scale 2 --fold`; a Mac window with `screencapture -o -w file.png`; mobile as its own capture at 390 x 844 (ground rule G11).
- **Status bar:** iOS Simulator `xcrun simctl status_bar booted override --time 9:41 --batteryLevel 100` (medium); Android through the System UI demo mode via `adb` (high [5]).
- **Device frame:** none by default; only when device or platform is part of the claim (case C7), then neutral (11.2). Never tilted, never with a glow (tell C3), never a floating phone as the anchor (tell V1). Screen video goes to a video skill.

### 3.6 Public-domain archives: check per image

1. **Read the status on the object page,** never from a search hit or file name (on Wikimedia the file page) (high [6]).
2. **Classify:** CC0 is free. Public Domain Mark, NoC-US (public domain only in the US, high [7]) and "No known restrictions": check the term of protection, when in doubt not for ads. CC BY, BY-SA: attribution following TASL. No statement: do not use.
3. **Term of protection:** check the term of protection in the country of use; works, simple photographs and photos of public-domain paintings follow different rules (Germany: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 12). Unknown author: only works older than 120 years (H).
4. **Rights next to copyright:** recognizable people (consent; EU and DACH: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 9), trademarks, implied endorsement; NASA logos never next to generated images (high [9]).
5. **Content:** historical terms can offend; a motif from the project world (ground rule G2), else tell V14. Treat the images of a series uniformly (duotone from the direction's colors, line); credits with the object ID.

### 3.7 SVG technique for illustration and diagram

One image world, the same across many surfaces (high for the icon rules [10], transfer H):

1. **One grid:** `viewBox` 24 (symbol) or 48 to 96 (illustration), half units, 1 unit margin.
2. **One stroke in display pixels:** `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke; stroke-width: 2` stays the same at every size (tested at 96 and 240 px).
3. **Round caps and corners,** radius 2 units; 2 units of space between parts.
4. **One fill color** from the direction on at most 25% of the figure; **one perspective.**
5. **Concrete objects from the project world** (tool, receipt, parcel), no figures with emotional expressions.
6. **Displayed at 96 px or more,** with a detail that fits only here; otherwise tell C10 or C12.

Agent errors (medium, the skill's own test): the fill sticks out past the outline (build it from the same shape or a `clipPath`); a flat fill band on an isometric body; look at every figure at 100%. **Diagrams:** label directly, gray for context, one accent (tell V17).

### 3.8 Type as image

A word, number or sign from the content (a date, a reading, a technical term), never a value word like "innovation". On social 15 to 25% of the height, cropping allowed; one function layer (sender, key sentence, action) at reading size. Build it as HTML text (`lang` set to the output language), export with `render.mjs`. Handwriting only as a real scan; doodles from `assets/doodles/` only in module M-HUM with an informal voice, with a reason from brand or content.

## 5 Best-form stand-in

If material is missing, the surface still looks finished: an honest stand-in in final form, final size and best form carries it until own material arrives; add the asset request (4). Own material without a face comes first (2). **The stand-in shows the claim of the surface** (the problem, the use, the result), not just the topic: for "capsules end up in the general waste" it shows capsules in the general waste, not a cup of coffee. If the same image fits every sentence about the topic, it is a topic photo. It never shows a fact that does not exist.

1. **Free photo** (Unsplash, Pexels, CC0, public-domain archives; license following 3.6 and 6, credits following 12) for material, ingredient, place, situation or problem, depending on the claim of the surface. Chosen by image brief (7), matched to the direction in crop, tonal range and color, no cliché (6). Quality like own material: sharp, at least 1.5 times the output size in pixels (never upscaled, check the dimensions before placing it), contemporary light, no filters over everything (sepia, vintage, grain as a substitute for quality); better one strong photo than three middling ones. For food and consumer goods it shows the moment of use in the situation the product is made for (for example freshly ground coffee in the portafilter). Never passed off as real employees, customers, speakers, your own location or the real product: no name, no caption, no "our" on the image.
2. **Faces** only as mood, never as a specific person (back, hands, crop, crowd, blur). For speakers, team, testimonials and founders never stock faces: a typographic or graphic anchor (name and role large, the quote as typography, a form from the shape set) plus an asset request.
3. **Product as illustration** from the description, construction data or a sketch: drawn (outline, area, section, exploded view), proportions from the source, never faked photorealistically and never generated; labeled in the review view and the wrap-up (below, Labeling). Only in illustration quality (light and shadow, material feel, consistent perspective, details such as seam, handle, lettering); a flat silhouette or an object built from basic shapes is clipart. The stroke technique of 3.7 applies to symbols and explanatory graphics; the product gets volume from one light direction (per face light, midtone, shadow) and a contact shadow, lettering as real text; check at 100% and at 360 px. If the drawing cannot achieve that, a free photo carries the proof by showing the claim of the surface (moment of use, problem, result); a photo of material or place only when the sentence on the surface talks about it. The product stays an asset request. The illustration has its own handwriting with an origin in the project (technique, stroke, texture); flat stock vector style with soft gradients and circle rings looks like clipart and misses the ship threshold; then a photo stand-in with appetite or touch carries the surface.
4. **Typographic anchor:** a word, number or sign from the content as an image (3.8).
5. **Data anchor:** a real number from the brief as a form; without a real number no placeholder figure, use another anchor form instead.
6. **Others:** own material without a face (hands, tools, place); an object from the project world as a cutout or SVG; the product interface as an HTML mockup from the brief (below); generated atmosphere or illustration after approval (8); change the direction when without material it would only be a costume.

**Product interface as a mockup from the brief** is design, not invention. If screenshots are missing, build the interface in HTML from the features in the brief ([craft.md](craft.md) 5.1, point 9), with the through-line as sample data: one case runs through the main steps. Only features from the brief, plus the frame controls of the category. Sample data is consistent and follows 3.5 (names from the target market, odd values with units), no real customer names, no Acme, no Lorem, no round fantasy numbers; on a persuasion surface the sample case is introduced as made up ([craft.md](craft.md) 5.1, point 9). Never passed off as a screenshot of the real product; screenshots following 3.5 stay an asset request.

**Final-form slot:** if no available stand-in shows the claim, the motif gets a final-form slot instead of an arbitrary topic photo: an area in the brand color in final form and final size with an outline or crop of the motif on it. Never gray, never the word PLACEHOLDER, never a label like "to be added" or "photo to follow", never a dashed frame, never the anchor of the whole work. The slot looks like the finished content in final form (for example the practice sign of the Weserbogen dental practice in outline, the name in the house typeface). That is better than a topic photo without a claim. Open facts of decision-critical sections are no slot: they are phrased with what is known, never as a label without a value, with the limits per surface and sequence from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) stage 4, point 6.

**No compositing:** never paint or blend the product into a foreign photo (a device pasted onto a stock photo, your own label retouched onto someone else's goods, a product drawing set into a photo scene), and never give it soft fake 3D (gradients, blur and gloss that fake physicality and look like plastic). Both read as a fake or an AI image. Instead, the whole scene as an illustration (point 3, volume from clearly set tonal steps) or a final-form slot. A visibly layered collage ([craft.md](craft.md) 5.3) is not compositing in this sense; the approved background plate behind a real product photo (8.1) and placing following 11.2 stay allowed within their limits.

**Repetition:** an image (photo, stand-in, illustration) appears at most twice in full form per sequence; every further appearance as a crop or detail with a new role ([craft.md](craft.md) 5.5). If the sequence needs more images, the asset plan (1) plans further motifs from the through-line instead of repeating one.

**Labeling** small and only in the review view (label "stand-in", "draft based on the brief" or "final-form slot", with source and wanted material) and in the wrap-up with the asset request; the presentation view stays free of it. Every free photo is in the credits; visible attribution is collected (12).

**Never an alternative:** initials circles, gray boxes, a gray placeholder area or the word PLACEHOLDER as the main motif (the final-form slot above is in color, shows the motif in outline or crop and is never the anchor of the whole work), an arbitrary topic photo that does not show the claim, rows of icons as the anchor (tells C8, C10), emoji (tell C9), stock faces as real employees, customers or speakers, generated people, logos or product UI (8.1), invented numbers, logos, quotes (tell E5; consistent sample data in a mockup from the brief is not invention), real customer names in sample data, flat figures as the main motif (tell V14). Stand-ins, final-form slots and what is lost go into the wrap-up.

## 7 Image brief

One line per image category before anything is searched for, photographed or generated; two to three categories per project (medium, IBM, GitLab). Fields: category; motif and action; perspective (one or two fixed camera positions); light and color temperature; depth of field; editing in one sentence; casting; crop and format; text zone in percent; prohibitions.

Example (EDI): reportage; a dispatcher plans the routes in the morning; over the shoulder, eye level; window light from the left; everything sharp; slightly desaturated; real employees with consent; 4:5 and 16:9; top 30% calm; no headset smile, no look into the camera.

## 8 Image generation (only after approval)

### 8.1 When

Only with three yeses: is material missing that the user cannot supply? Do archive, stock and self-building fail at the motif? Does the image make no claim to authenticity? (H)

- **Suitable:** a background plate behind a real product photo, as long as the product does not look different or better (medium [13]); atmosphere without a concrete place; still life as an editorial image (case B3); illustration only as a series with an anchor image (a single piece is tell V20); moodboard and sketch, never delivered; outpainting of a real photo only when labeled.
- **Never:** people as customers, team, testimonial or audience; the product being sold; product UI; logo, text, data, chart; an event or a real place as "our place"; "rescuing" a direction without material (downgrade it instead).

### 8.2 Approval request

Image tools (tool check in stage 0) cost quota. No call without approval; it applies only to the named motif:

> **For [surface] I propose a generated image,** because [alternatives] fail at the motif. Motif: [image brief in one sentence]. Tool: [service, model], cost: [if known]. Labeling: [yes or no, where]. Without generation I will do [alternative] and lose [what]. Approve?

Autonomous or no user available: do not generate, build the alternative, propose it in the wrap-up.

### 8.3 Prompt against the AI look

The AI look is the model's default: backlight, bokeh, smooth skin, centered composition, high gloss. Build the prompt from the image brief (7) plus purpose, material, color from the tokens and empty space; scene, motif, details and constraints in full sentences (high [14]). Store the prompt as a file.

| Instead of | Better |
|---|---|
| "cinematic, golden hour" | one light source with a direction ("overcast window light from the left") |
| 85 mm f/1.4 | eye level, 35 to 50 mm, f/5.6 to f/8, all in focus |
| "premium, sleek", smooth perfection | materials and signs of wear ("worn edges, visible pores") |
| centered hero motif | motif in a third, the emptiness named ("the left half is an empty wall") |
| "no cars" (FLUX has no negative prompt) | positive: "an empty street" |
| "vibrant", "8k, masterpiece", headline in the image | color in words from the tokens; type from the system on top |

GPT Image: state "no text, no logos, no watermark" explicitly; Midjourney `--style raw`, low `--stylize` (medium). Per round exactly one change plus "keep everything else the same" (high [14]). Example (STI, blog header):

> A quiet still life photograph for a blog header, text on the left: one unglazed stoneware cup on a pale oak table in the right third. Overcast window light from the left, soft shadow to the right. Eye level, 50 mm, f/8, all in focus. Visible clay texture, small scratches on the table. Muted, slightly cool colors; the left half is an empty off-white wall. No text, no logos, no watermark, no people.

### 8.4 Series

1. **Anchor image first,** have it approved; it is the reference for all further images ("same style, same light, do not redesign") (high [14]).
2. **One prompt frame:** only the motif changes; light, camera, color and prohibitions word for word the same.
3. **Style references only from own material,** never third-party artists or studios (ground rule G8).
4. **Generation seed** (not the random seed of the direction choice) only as a supplement, never switch off watermarks for it; on drift a new session with the anchor image.
5. **Check:** `$PY $S/pixels.py sheet sheet.png image-*.png --cols 3 --labels` and `palette` per image; adjust or discard outliers.

### 8.5 Checking for AI artifacts

Look at every image, stock included, for 10 seconds at original size, then at 25% (medium [15]). **Anatomy:** fingers, teeth, ears, hidden limbs, uneven catchlights in the eyes. **Style:** waxy skin, high gloss, HDR edges, perfect symmetry, light without a source. **Function:** character mush instead of lettering, logo-like marks, handles without an attachment, cables into nothing. **Physics:** shadows against the light, reflections without a source, floating objects. **Context:** power sockets, signs, license plates, season. **Stock aesthetic:** everyone smiles into the camera, flawlessly empty rooms (tells V8, V9).

Crop it away, regenerate with one change or discard; after two failed attempts build the alternative. R3 gets this list as a rubric.

### 8.6 Post-processing and prohibitions

Crop by composition, one tonal range per series, saturation following the direction's strategy; upscale only to the resolution needed (3.3), then check for invented details. **Store the original with C2PA unchanged** (`assets/original/`): re-saving with Pillow drops the APP11 block with C2PA from a JPEG (medium, the skill's own test). On export set the IPTC source type (high [16]; tested for JPG, WebP, PNG):

```bash
$PY $S/ai_label.py image.png image.jpg  # default trainedAlgorithmicMedia: fully generated
$PY $S/ai_label.py photo.png photo.webp --type compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia  # inpainting, outpainting
$PY $S/ai_label.py m.png m-out.png --type compositeSynthetic  # compositing with a generated part
$PY $S/ai_label.py --check image.jpg
```

**Forbidden, even with approval** (in addition to 8.1): real people or look-alike faces; the name or style of living artists in the prompt; the product better than in reality; uploading photos of real people as a reference, your own product photos only to services with checked terms; removing watermarks or provenance data; generated output as a logo or own asset (ground rule G3), because copyright protection of generated output is not assured (Germany: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 12).

### 8.7 Labeling and services

Law: [compliance.md](compliance.md) 3 (AI content); EU: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 13. Design rules (H, after [13][18]):

1. **Classify:** could pass as real (also an invented person, an embellished product): label it. A real product in front of a generated background without deception: no duty, note it in the credits. Stylized illustration: label when in doubt.
2. **Label in the image** (survives sharing): "AI-generated" in the output language (German wording: [markets/german.md](markets/german.md) 2), outside the platform's no-go zones, 4.5:1, legibly large (web from 12 px, social from 24 px at 1080 px width, slide from 18 px at 1080 px height, print from 7 pt); at first contact, not only in the footer.
3. **In addition** alt text or caption, the platform switch, the IPTC source type; in the wrap-up every labeling and every deliberate non-labeling with its reason.

**Services:** read the terms of use before first use and enter them with date in the credits. As of 2026-10-08 (medium): indemnification against third-party claims only in enterprise plans; Midjourney publishes images except in stealth mode.

## 9 Illustration

Only when it shows what a photo or product view cannot: a process, the inside, the future, a metaphor (medium), or as a stand-in for a missing product photo (5, point 3).

**Generic warning:** free flat sets deliver figures with overlong limbs and solid-color areas, the caricature of DRW (tell V14). Risk very high: unDraw (free, no attribution, no AI training [19]), Storyset; high: Humaaans, Open Peeps (CC0), Blush; medium: Open Doodles. Never as the main motif; if unavoidable, with the brand's own stroke and palette and real material.

Better: archive illustration as a series (HER, EDI); SVG following 3.7; data form (DAT); forms from the logo or a primitive with a documented derivation (RUL, ONE); commissioned illustration with attribution, the character in the lead role instead of as tile decoration (tell V20); generated illustration only as an approved series (8.4). Never imitate living illustrators or studios (ground rule G8).

## 10 Icons

Checked 2026-10-07 (high [19]). Low echo: **Phosphor** (MIT, 6 weights, at 24 px Light 1.125, Regular 1.5, Bold 2.25 px), **Iconoir** (MIT, 1.5). Medium: **Tabler** (MIT, 2), **Remix Icon** (own license, never as logo, trademark, app icon). High: **Lucide** (ISC and MIT; `generic-check.mjs` detects its classes), **Heroicons** (MIT), **Material Symbols** (Apache 2.0), **Font Awesome Free** (CC BY 4.0, attribution required). SF Symbols only on Apple platforms, never in a logo or app icon. SVG via jsDelivr (`npm/@phosphor-icons/core@2.1.1/assets/light/<name>-light.svg`); on live sites store them locally.

1. **Function signs** for navigation, status, action, wayfinding. In marketing, instead of icon rows: a product crop, data form, photo or the brand's own pictogram system.
2. **One set, one style;** fill only for the active state.
3. **Size:** 16 px next to 13 to 14 px text, 20 next to 16 to 17, 24 next to 18 to 20; target area at least 24 x 24 CSS px, primary action 44 px (ground rule G6).
4. **Stroke matched to the type:** text 300 to Light (1.25 to 1.5), 400 to Regular (1.5 to 1.75), 600 to 700 to Bold (2); for Lucide and Tabler set `stroke-width`.
5. **Technique:** inline with `currentColor`; decorative `aria-hidden="true"`, otherwise visible text or `aria-label`; never an icon or color as the only carrier of meaning.
6. **Generic:** icon tile above identical cards (tells C8, V3); a standard set as the only imagery (tell C10); rocket, light bulb, sparkles for AI; emoji (tell C9).
7. **License:** MIT and ISC: notice in the source code; keep the Font Awesome comment. Never remove Lucide classes to get around the check; in the wrap-up "intentional".

## 11 Textures, 3D, editing

### 11.1 Textures and 3D

Sources: Poly Haven and ambientCG (CC0 [19]), Openverse with `license=cc0,pdm`, Kenney (CC0, do not use the logo), Smithsonian 3D (CC0), scans of your own (module M-HUM).

1. Texture only as material of the brand world (paper, print, fabric, concrete); grain over a gradient is generic, the same grain on everything is tell V25.
2. Opacity under 10% as a start, check at 100%; text on top 4.5:1 at the least favorable spot. `feTurbulence` only as a last resort.
3. 3D only for the real product (render, scan, exploded view) or spatial data (module M-SPA); clay icons, glass and chrome shapes, blobs are generic (tell V1).

### 11.2 Editing, cutouts, device frames (local)

Everything offline; edit photos of real people only locally. Commands: [scripts/README.md](../scripts/README.md).

- **Cutout** for breakout and people compositions following 3.4, shadow from one light direction; a cutout does not replace the consent of the people shown (EU and DACH: [markets/eu-dach-compliance.md](markets/eu-dach-compliance.md) 9).
- **Mockup in use instead of in a vacuum:** `$PY $S/perspective.py subject.png photo.jpg "x1,y1 x2,y2 x3,y3 x4,y4" out.jpg --shadow 0.25` (corners from top left, clockwise; tested 2026-10-08); the background only one of the brand's own photos or, solely to show your own design in use (poster on a wall, ad in a bus shelter), a CC0 photo; never insert the client's product into a foreign photo (5, No compositing).
- **Web export** in 1x and 2x, small without visible loss, dimensions in the HTML (H).
- **Photo on a flat page color:** bake a blend into the file instead of using `mix-blend-mode`. In one Safari window a photo's black box covered the text (one screenshot, 2026-11-02; a WebKit snapshot did not show it). To hide a photo's ground, write the page color into the image, as its minimum value on a dark page (Pillow: `ImageChops.lighter`), as its maximum on a light one (`ImageChops.darker`), and keep text above the image in the stacking order.
- rembg loads a model from the network: only after approval. Do not assume ImageMagick or exiftool.

**Neutral frame:** phone 360 px, border 10 px, radii 52 and 42 px, #16181a, screen 390:844; browser 760 px, bar 36 px, a real domain; no camera island, traffic-light dots, tilt or glow; a real render inside. Apple bezels only for app marketing on Apple platforms, unchanged, with the app running (medium [20]).

## 12 Credits rule

Every output gets `CREDITS.md` and `credits.json` in the same folder, created when the asset goes in. Template, fields, duties per license, attribution and credits gate: [templates/credits.md](../templates/credits.md). Without complete credits nothing is finished.

**Collected instead of under every image:** image credits are collected at the medium's usual place following [templates/credits.md](../templates/credits.md) 4 (in a deck on the last slide, on the web on an "Image credits" page, on social in the caption), not as a line under every image in a talk. Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay and CC0 require no attribution. CC BY and BY-SA require it but allow any form reasonable for the medium and context (CC BY 4.0, section 3(a)(2); high [21]); collected is enough when every attribution can be matched to its image (slide or page number, image title). If a license or contract requires attribution at the image, that applies. The labeling of generated images stays at the image (8.7).
